# Ad Hoc Analysis - Stakeholder Response

Request: "Is heavy discounting reducing overall profitability? Which product sub-categories should we reconsider discount levels for?"
Prepared by: Dunith Athukorala 
Date range analyzed: 2014–2017

## Approach
Order-line data was grouped into four discount bands, No Discount, Low (1–20%), Medium (21–40%), and High (40%+), and total profit was compared across bands. Average discount and total profit were then compared at the sub-category level to identify where discounting is having the largest negative effect.

## Findings
- Total profit declines steadily as discount band increases. Profit is highest with no discount applied and falls sharply once discounts pass the 20% mark.
- Bookcases is the sub-category of greatest concern. It carries a 21% average discount and is the only sub-category in the entire dataset running a net loss overall (-3,472.56 total profit).
- Binders carries the highest average discount of any sub-category (37%) and, while still profitable overall (30,221.76), represents the largest exposure if discount levels increase or if margins on individual orders continue narrowing.
- All other sub-categories remain net-positive at current discount levels.

## Recommendation
- Cap Bookcases discounts at 15% or below, or review its base pricing, the current discount level is not sustainable for this sub-category.
- Set a monitoring threshold on Binders discount levels, given its outsized exposure relative to every other sub-category.
No immediate action needed on the remaining sub-categories, discounting there is not yet eroding profitability at current levels, though the same discount-band review should be repeated periodically (e.g. quarterly) to catch any future drift.
